# Materials, quotes, measuring and checklists (quick reference)

All numbers are common rules of thumb, rounded. The product label and the shop have the final word. Show every calculation in one line so the user can check it.

## Material quantities

### Paint (walls)
`wall area = perimeter × height − doors − windows`
`litres = wall area × coats ÷ coverage`
- Door ≈ 1.6–2.0 m², window ≈ 1.5–2.5 m² (use real sizes if given).
- Coverage: about **8–12 m² per litre per coat**; use the number on the tin. Use 10 if unknown and say so.
- Coats: 2 (3 for a big colour change, dark over light, or fresh plaster after primer).
- Ceiling: floor area ÷ coverage × coats, separately.
- Example: room 3.2 × 3.6 m, height 2.7 m → perimeter 13.6 m × 2.7 = 36.7 m² − door 1.8 − window 2.0 = 32.9 m² × 2 coats ÷ 10 = **6.6 L → buy 7–8 L** (tins come in fixed sizes).

### Floor: laminate, parquet, vinyl
`m² to buy = floor area × 1.05–1.10` (5% straight laying, 10% for many corners; 15% diagonal / herringbone). Round up to whole packs (m² per pack is on the box). Add underlay for the same area and skirting = perimeter − door widths + 5%.

### Tiles
`m² to buy = area × 1.10` (straight); × 1.15 for diagonal, large-format or patterned tiles. Wall tiles: wall area minus openings. Buy all tiles from one batch (same batch code) and keep a few spares. Grout and adhesive: use the amounts on the bag for the tile size; ask the shop.

### Wallpaper (standard roll 0.53 m × 10.05 m)
1. `strips needed = (perimeter − wide openings) ÷ 0.53`, round up.
2. `strips per roll = 10.05 ÷ (height + 0.10 + pattern repeat)`, round **down**.
3. `rolls = strips needed ÷ strips per roll`, round up, + 1 spare.
Example: perimeter 13.6 m − door 0.8 = 12.8 ÷ 0.53 = 24.2 → 25 strips; height 2.7 + 0.1 + repeat 0 = 2.8 → 10.05 ÷ 2.8 = 3 strips per roll; 25 ÷ 3 = 8.3 → **9 rolls + 1 spare = 10**. Wide rolls (1.06 m): use 1.06 in step 1.

## Budget and quotes

- Contingency: **10–15%** for cosmetic work, **15–20%** in old buildings or when walls/floors are opened.
- Rough share for a full cosmetic renovation: labour 35–50%, materials 50–65%. Say prices vary by city and year; never present them as quotes.
- **Get 3 written quotes** for the same scope. Compare line by line, not just totals.
- **Quote red flags:** no itemised list (only one total); no materials list or brand; big cash payment up front; "no need for a contract"; price far below the others; refuses to name who does electrics or gas; can start "tomorrow" on a big job.
- **Payment tied to finished stages:** e.g. 10–20% start (materials), then after rough-in, after plaster/screed, after finishes; keep **10%** until the snag list is fixed. Photos of hidden work (pipes, cables) before it is covered.
- **Contractor checklist:** past work you can see; references you can call; written scope, dates and price; who buys materials; warranty; who cleans and removes rubbish; licensed electrician and gas fitter named.

## Measuring guide (what to measure, in cm)

1. Each wall at floor height and at 1 m (old flats are rarely square). Write the smaller number.
2. Ceiling height in 2–3 places.
3. Doors: width and height of the opening; which way it swings.
4. Windows: width, height, **sill height** from the floor, radiator below it.
5. Sockets, switches, radiators, pipes, gas meter, electric panel: distance from a corner and height.
6. Diagonals of the room (equal = square room).
7. Take a phone photo of each wall with a tape measure in view.

## "Will it fit?" (before buying big items)

Check the **smallest** gap on the way in: building door, lift (width × depth × height, and the diagonal), stair turn, flat door, corridor corner, room door. A sofa fits through a door if its **depth or height** (the smaller one) is less than the door width; for a corner, also check the diagonal. Standing an item on end only helps if its footprint on end (the two other sizes) fits the lift floor or door. Ask if legs or back come off. Mattresses can bend; bed frames and wardrobes often come flat-packed.

## Living through the works and long-lead items

- Phases with **no water**: plumbing rough-in, bathroom tiling. **No gas or cooking**: kitchen work. **Dust everywhere**: demolition, sanding, plaster. Plan where to stay or which room to seal.
- Safe pause points (budget runs out): after rough-in + plaster (walls closed, safe), or after one room is fully finished. Never pause with open wiring, open pipes or no door locks.
- **Order early:** custom windows and doors, kitchen units, built-in wardrobes, made-to-size glass, imported tiles. Ask the lead time at the first visit.

## Renter-friendly mode (no drilling, deposit-safe)

- Get the landlord's written OK for anything that changes walls, floors or fixtures.
- Reversible: removable hooks and strips (test on a hidden spot), tension rods, freestanding shelves, plug-in lamps and LED strips, rugs over old floors, peel-and-stick only where the landlord agrees, curtains on tension rods or existing rails.
- Photograph the flat on move-in day (date visible) to protect the deposit.
- Keep the old fittings (lampshades, curtains) to put back.

## Moving-in / first-week checklist (often forgotten)

- Before: measure the flat (see above), check the lift and door sizes, book the move, read the meters (water, gas, electricity) with photos.
- Day 1: change or check the locks; find the water valve, gas valve, electric panel; test smoke detector (buy one if none); check every socket with a phone charger.
- Week 1 basics: bin bags, cleaning kit, toilet paper, light bulbs, extension lead with a fuse, first-aid kit, door mat, shower curtain, curtains or blinds for the bedroom, a tool kit (screwdriver set, tape measure, level).
- Buy order by urgency: **sleep** (mattress) → **light and privacy** (bulbs, curtains) → **cook and clean** → **storage** → decor.
